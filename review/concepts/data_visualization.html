Data Visualization

Playfair invented the charts, Nightingale proved they move policy, Anscombe proved you can't skip them — and Tufte wrote the honesty rules.

1 · Where it came from

William Playfair — Scottish engineer, sometime secret agent — invented the line chart, bar chart, and pie chart in the 1780s-90s, essentially alone, to argue about trade and debt. Florence Nightingale weaponized the idea: her 1858 rose diagrams showed Parliament that soldiers were dying of hospital hygiene, not wounds — and the charts changed policy where reports had failed.

The modern conscience of the field is Frank Anscombe, whose 1973 quartet (below) proved that summary statistics can hide what a picture reveals — and Edward Tufte, who gave us the vocabulary of chart honesty: data-ink, chartjunk, and the lie factor.

The four classic lies: truncated y-axis (2% looks like a landslide) · cherry-picked time windows · area/size distortion (double the radius = 4× the area readers see) · missing denominators (“300 complaints!” — out of how many orders?).

3 · Anscombe’s Quartet — same statistics, four different truths

ALL FOUR datasets:  mean x = 9.0 · mean y = 7.50 · correlation = 0.816 · fitted line y = 3.00 + 0.50x
I: honest linear · II: a curve no line should model · III: one outlier owns the slope · IV: one point manufactures the whole correlation. Identical summaries — which is why EDA charts come BEFORE statistics you trust.

3b · The truncation lie — flip the axis

Same two stores, 98.1% vs 99.3% on-time. Truncated: B looks twice as good. Zero-based: the honest, boring truth. Uncheck the box in front of the class.

✅ Self-check — no internet needed

Q1. Anscombe's four datasets share identical means, correlation, and fitted line. The lesson is:

Identical summaries hide a curve, an outlier-owned slope, and a manufactured correlation — only the picture tells them apart.

Q2. A bar chart of 98.1% vs 99.3% on-time rates starts its axis at 96%. The chart is:

Bars encode length; truncation makes a 1.2-point gap fill the frame. Tufte's lie factor ≈ 32 here.

Q3. Your Pareto shows two categories covering 71% of complaints. The right title is:

A deliverable chart's title states the FINDING, not the topic — the reader gets the message even if they read nothing else.
