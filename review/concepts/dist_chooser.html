Choosing the Distribution

The thirty-second decision that determines whether everything downstream is right: measuring or counting — and which counting.

The one question that starts everything

Are you measuring or counting? A weight, a time, a length — measuring, continuous, curve country. “How many?” — counting, whole numbers, bar country. Choosing the wrong country is the original sin of applied statistics; no formula downstream can save you from it.

The decision path

ask yourselfif yes →Excel family
Measuring something continuous, one symmetric hump? Normal (μ, σ)NORM.DIST / NORM.INV
Counting successes out of a FIXED number of yes/no trials? Binomial (n, p)BINOM.DIST
Counting events per window with no fixed n, steady average rate? Poisson (λ)POISSON.DIST
Small sample (n < 30), σ unknown, working with means? t-distribution (df)T.DIST / T.INV
Waiting TIME between Poisson events? Exponential (λ)EXPON.DIST
Judging category tables or variance ratios (not modeling data)? The referees: χ² and FCHISQ.DIST.RT / F.DIST.RT
Every value in a range equally likely (raffles, simulation inputs)? Uniform (a, b) — the know-nothing baseline: mean (a+b)/2, no Excel function needed — probability is just width-fraction: P(x₁<X<x₂) = (x₂−x₁)/(b−a)=(x2-x1)/(b-a)

Fingerprints — quick checks on your own data

distributionfingerprintred flag it isn't
Normal~68 / 95 / 99.7% inside ±1σ / 2σ / 3σskew, twin peaks, hard limits nearby
Binomialmean ≈ np, variance ≈ np(1−p)p drifting, trials influencing each other
Poissonmean ≈ variancevariance ≫ mean (clumping) or drifting rate

Bridges between them

Binomial → Poisson: n large (>20), p small (<0.05) → use λ = np. Rare defects in big batches.
Everything → Normal: the Central Limit Theorem — AVERAGES of many observations go bell-shaped no matter what the raw data does. It's why the normal owns inference.
The habit to build: before touching a formula, say out loud what one data point IS (a measurement? a count out of n? a count per window?), then check the fingerprint against your sample. Thirty seconds of this prevents entire wrong analyses.

✅ Self-check — no internet needed

Q1. Walk the path: 'Did average checkout time improve after the redesign?' (large samples, numeric outcome). The chain is:

First identify the DATA TYPE (numeric measurement), then the question (compare means), then the referee (z/t) and its CI. The path is always type → question → tool

Q2. 'Did the redesign change the SHARE of visitors who buy?' The chain is:

Share-who-did-it is binomial's yes/no world; the significance machinery is the proportion test. (A 2x2 chi-square would agree — they're cousins here — but the proportion CI is the business-readable output)

Q3. 'Are complaint COUNTS down since the new packaging?' The chain is:

No fixed n, events per interval → Poisson, and the question 'is the new rate really lower?' is a rate test. Choosing binomial here invents a trial count that doesn't exist
