Data Mining

Finding patterns that are real, useful, and previously unknown — with lift as the judge and two oaths before acting.

1 · Where it came from

Retailers invented the field before it had a name. The legend — told in every analytics course, possibly embellished by every teller — is the mid-90s “beer and diapers” discovery: transaction mining at a US chain reportedly found the two purchased together on weekday evenings. True or not, the legend stuck because the LESSON is true: patterns worth money hide in ordinary transaction logs, and no human hypothesis would have gone looking for that one.

The discipline’s honest name was coined in the database community: knowledge discovery in databases. Mining sits INSIDE your decision workflow (DDDM) — the business question was framed Tuesday of Week 1, the data integrated Wednesday, explored yesterday. Mining is a middle phase. It suggests; the evaluation phase decides.

2 · The mathematics of a pattern

support(A→B) = count(A and B)N  ·  confidence(A→B) = count(A and B)count(A)  ·  lift(A→B) = confidence(A→B)P(B)
support — how often the pair appears at all (is it worth caring?) · confidence — when A is bought, how often B follows · lift — how much MORE likely B is, given A, than B is on its own
Lift is the referee: lift = 1 means A tells you nothing about B · lift > 1 means genuine association · lift < 1 means A buyers AVOID B. High confidence with lift ≈ 1 is the classic rookie trap — everyone buys bread, so “A → bread” always looks confident and means nothing.
The two families: SUPERVISED — you have labels (churned / stayed) and predict them: classification & regression. UNSUPERVISED — no labels; find structure: clustering & association rules. The business question picks the family: “will THIS customer churn?” is supervised; “what kinds of customers exist?” is unsupervised.

Slide “both” down and watch lift cross 1.0 — the moment a “pattern” becomes noise. Then push salsa’s solo count up: confidence holds but lift collapses. Confidence flatters; lift judges.

The two oaths before acting on any mined pattern: ① Correlation is not causation — ice cream and drownings share a cause (summer); patterns are leads for investigation or A/B tests (Week 3’s machinery), not laws. ② Beware overfitting — a model that memorizes last quarter’s noise is brilliant on data it has seen and useless on data it hasn’t. Hold out data; judge there. Robust beats clever.

5 · When to reach for it — and when not

Mine when…Don’t mine when…
You have volume: thousands of transactions/rowsn is tiny — you’ll “find” patterns in noise
The workflow’s question is framed and the data exploredNobody can say what decision a found pattern would change

✅ Self-check — no internet needed

Q1. A rule shows confidence 0.92 but lift 1.01. The honest reading is:

High confidence with lift ≈ 1 means B's base rate is high; conditioning on A adds nothing. Lift, not confidence, judges.

Q2. 'Customers who bought X churned 3× more often.' Before acting, the analyst should:

Mining found co-movement, not cause; a shared driver (price tier? acquisition channel?) may explain both.

Q3. Your model scores 99.5% on the data it trained on and 61% on new data. This is:

A large train-holdout gap is the overfitting signature; simpler, regularized, or better-validated models generalize.
