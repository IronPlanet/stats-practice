The six branches

“Can I trust this table?”

Nothing downstream survives a broken join or the wrong unit of analysis. This is where most real errors are made, and the cheapest place to catch them.

Not sure which branch?

Work down this list and stop at the first one that matches what you are actually doing.

  1. Are you still assembling or cleaning the data, or unsure what one row means? → Branch 1
  2. Do you need to summarise or picture a column before deciding anything? → Branch 2
  3. Do you need a probability — how often, how long, how many? → Branch 3
  4. Do you have two or more groups, or a claim to check against evidence? → Branch 4
  5. Do you want to know what predicts an outcome, or to forecast one? → Branch 5
  6. Do you have to choose between options with uncertain payoffs? → Branch 6

How the branches connect

They are not independent, and the arrows matter more than the boxes.

1 → 2 → everything. A test run on a bad join or a mis-specified unit of analysis produces a confident, precise, wrong answer — and nothing later in the chain will warn you. Branch 1 is not preliminary work; it is where the conclusion is decided.

2 → 3 → 4. The shape of a column tells you which distribution is plausible, and the distribution tells you which test is legitimate. Skipping straight to a t-test is choosing a distribution without admitting it.

4 → 5. A significant difference between groups and a fitted model answer different questions. “These two groups differ” is branch 4. “Here is how much of the outcome each factor accounts for” is branch 5.

5 → 6. A coefficient is not a decision. Branch 6 is where an estimate, its uncertainty and the cost of being wrong turn into a recommendation.

One warning about the map

Branch 4 has 16 entries and branch 6 has 3. That imbalance is real and it is worth noticing. Most statistics teaching — this course included — spends its effort on deciding whether a difference is real, and comparatively little on what to do once you know. In working life the proportions are closer to reversed.
