Descriptive Statistics

Mean against median, spread that survives a tail, and the shape diagnostics that tell you when a single summary is lying.

1 · Where it came from

The arithmetic mean arrived from astronomy. Eighteenth-century observers took the same measurement many times, got different numbers every time, and averaged them because errors were believed to cancel. That logic is sound — when the errors are symmetric. It quietly fails the moment a distribution has a long tail, which is most business data.

The median is the older idea, and the more stubborn one. Francis Galton popularised it in the 1880s precisely because it ignores how far away an extreme value sits — only that it is on one side. Karl Pearson added the coefficient of variation in 1896 so that spread could be compared across variables measured in different units, and the words “skewness” and “kurtosis” are his too.

The useful question is never “what is the average?” It is “which summary survives this data's shape?”

2 · The mathematics

x̄ = 1n ∑i=1n xi      s2 = ∑(xi − x̄)2n − 1      s = √s2
Why n − 1? The deviations are measured from x̄, which was itself estimated from the same data. One degree of freedom is already spent, so dividing by n understates the spread. This is the difference between Excel's STDEV.S and STDEV.P — use .S unless you genuinely hold every member of the population.
CV = sx̄      IQR = Q3 − Q1
CV is unitless, so it compares spread across variables on different scales — minutes against dollars against headcount. IQR is the width of the middle 50% and, unlike s, is unaffected by anything beyond the quartiles.
g1 = n(n−1)(n−2) ∑ (xi − x̄s)3      g2 = n(n+1)(n−1)(n−2)(n−3) ∑ (xi − x̄s)4 − 3(n−1)2(n−2)(n−3)
g1 (skewness) — asymmetry. Positive means a right tail. g2 (excess kurtosis) — tailedness, already centred so a normal curve scores 0. These are exactly Excel's SKEW and KURT; other software sometimes reports the population versions, which differ at small n.
Read g2 backwards and it becomes useful. A negative excess kurtosis is not a “light tail” in practice — it is usually a flat or two-humped shape. A uniform distribution scores −1.2. If a business column comes back near −1, suspect you have stacked two different populations in one column, and that neither the mean nor the median of the pooled column describes anything real.

3 · Drag the outlier

Eleven deliveries plus one that went wrong. Move the twelfth and watch which summaries hold still. The dataset starts at the real figures: 42, 47, 51, 53, 56, 58, 61, 64, 68, 73, 79, 128 minutes.

mean = 65.00  ·  median = 59.50  ·  s = 22.52  ·  CV = 0.346  ·  upper fence = 94.38  ·  beyond the fence
The mean chases the point; the median does not move at all until the point crosses the middle of the data. The gap between them is the skew.

5 · Assumptions, and when these break

SummaryQuietly assumesBreaks when
Meanroughly symmetric, no extreme tailskewed or heavy-tailed data — report the median beside it
s and CVa single populationthe column stacks sub-groups; compute within groups instead
CVa ratio scale with x̄ well away from 0the mean nears zero (CV explodes) or the data can go negative (CV is meaningless)
1.5×IQR fenceunimodal, one populationmixtures — it then flags an entire legitimate sub-group as outliers
Kurtosisenough data to estimate a 4th momentsmall n — g2 is wildly unstable below about n = 30, and undefined below n = 4
The fence is a convention, not a test. It has no significance level and no null hypothesis. It says “look at this point”, never “delete this point.” A flagged value gets investigated and, if it is real, kept — with a sensitivity analysis showing the conclusion both ways.
Censoring and ceiling effects. When an instrument, form or policy cannot record past a boundary, values pile up on it. The variance there is artificially near zero and the mean is a lower bound, not an estimate. Repeated identical values at the top or bottom of a column are the fingerprint — and no summary statistic will warn you.

✅ Self-check — no internet needed

Q1. A delivery-time column has mean 64.2 and median 58.0. What do you conclude before running anything else?

Mean above median is the signature of a right tail. Neither number is wrong; they answer different questions, and the gap between them is the skew. No test is needed to see it.

Q2. A column of customer satisfaction scores returns an excess kurtosis of −1.06. The most likely explanation is:

Excess kurtosis near −1 signals a flat or two-humped shape (a uniform scores −1.2). Heavy tails would drive g₂ positive, and a normal scores 0. Split the column by a plausible grouping variable and summarise within groups.

Q3. Which comparison is the coefficient of variation appropriate for?

CV = s/x̄ needs a ratio scale with the mean well away from zero. Negative values make the ratio meaningless (B), and a mean near zero makes it explode (C). Unitless does not mean universal.

Q4. A value falls beyond the 1.5×IQR upper fence. The correct next step is:

The fence is a convention with no significance level, so there is no p-value to report and nothing has been proven false. It flags a point for a human to look at. Deleting or overwriting real data because it is inconvenient is the failure mode the rule exists to prevent.
